2Why We Have This Little Agreement

It exists for happy reasons: so everyone shares the same plan, feels respected, and can focus on the music. In short, it helps us all agree on what we'll do, who's responsible for what, how we'll stay in touch before and during the event, and how we'll handle anything unexpected — calmly and kindly. It's here to prevent confusion before it starts and to protect the time and dignity of everyone involved: your team, our musicians and volunteers, and the community we're all there to serve. It's not about formality or distance — it's about starting with shared understanding so the day feels smooth and joyful.

7The Space We'll Need

We're easy to please — we just need a spot that's safe, clean, and comfortable for the music and for chatting with neighbors. In practice that means:

  • Enough room for our musicians, instruments, stands, cases, outreach table, and cameras, without crowding
  • Clear of fire exits, narrow walkways, kitchens, and busy doorways where folks might bump us or our gear
  • The exact performance and table spots identified before we start setting up
  • Available to us for the whole setup, performance, outreach, filming, and breakdown window
  • Protected from rain, harsh sun, wind, heat, or cold if we're outdoors
  • A clear line of sight for our cameras, and room for volunteers to chat with attendees safely

If the spot turns out too small, unsafe, overcrowded, or very different from what we pictured, our event lead may make small, good-faith adjustments on-site — shorten, relocate, or simplify — and only as a last resort step back. We'll always talk it through with you first.

14Staying in Touch Before the Day

Verbal chats are wonderful — but let's put the final details in writing too (a quick email thread is perfect), so all three of us remember the same plan. If anything important changes as the day approaches, a short note to the contacts above keeps everyone in sync.

15On the Day — Keeping Communication Clear

To keep things calm, day-of requests flow like this:

Organizer / Venue Host  →  Harmony 4 All event lead  →  our musicians & volunteers

Please send requests through our event lead rather than to individual musicians or our youth volunteers — it prevents mixed messages and uncomfortable moments. The one exception is an immediate safety concern, where of course anyone should speak up right away. If something urgent comes up, just find our lead.

16Respect & Courtesy, Both Ways

We treat your team, your space, and your guests with patience and respect — and we warmly ask the same for our musicians and volunteers, especially our young people. In practice, that means we all agree not to:

  • Interrupt musicians mid-performance (unless it's a safety thing)
  • Give conflicting instructions to individual players or volunteers
  • Pressure anyone to continue past the agreed time
  • Move or touch instruments, gear, cameras, or belongings without asking our lead
  • Publicly criticize or confront our musicians or volunteers
  • Change our role without a word to our event lead
  • Treat our team's time or presence as informal just because the music is a community gift

If a concern comes up, let's raise it calmly through our contacts. If something truly disrespectful continues after we've gently flagged it, we may pause or wind down — but we'd always much rather simply talk it through.

17If Plans Change on the Day

Community events shift, and that's okay! For bigger changes, just check with our event lead first so we can make it work together. Bigger changes include things like:

  • Moving us to a different spot, or changing our performance time or length
  • Relocating, shrinking, or removing our 6-ft outreach table
  • Blocking or changing our agreed camera spots
  • Asking for a different kind of performance, or switching between background and featured music
  • Removing access to electricity
  • Changing how many musicians or volunteers we bring
  • Asking us to join political, campaign, fundraising, or public-messaging activity we hadn't discussed
  • Asking us to stay beyond the agreed time, or to set up in a space that's too small or unsafe

We'll always try to say yes where we safely can — a quick conversation lets us adapt happily.

18If We Ever Need to Pause or Step Back

We truly hope it never comes up. But so everyone knows ahead of time, there are a few situations where our event lead or leadership might pause, shorten, relocate, or — only as a last resort — step back: if the space is unsafe or too small; if the setup is very different from what we agreed; if musicians are interrupted again and again; if our team or volunteers are treated unkindly; if our gear or belongings are at risk; if there's no clear contact; if things become chaotic or hostile; if we're asked to do things outside this plan; or if something genuinely unexpected affects safety, dignity, or our ability to perform.

And for anything not spelled out here — because no plan can foresee everything — let's simply handle it in good faith, using the spirit of this whole agreement: safety, respect, clear communication, transparency, and care for our musicians, volunteers, and the community we're serving.

19Photos & Video

We love to document our visits — for our records, social media, grant reports, and community storytelling. To capture the performance well, we may set up a small camera arrangement around the stage:

  • One camera straight on (about a 90° angle to the stage), capturing the full performance
  • One camera at roughly 45° from one side
  • One camera at roughly 45° from the other side
  • One roaming camera, held by a volunteer, for candid audience reactions and outreach-table moments

We'll discuss camera placement with you before the event and confirm the exact spots before setup begins, always keeping everything clear of walkways. Once cameras are set, we just ask that they aren't moved or unplugged without a quick word to our event lead. If our branded backdrop is set up behind the performers, it also gives a lovely, consistent background for the footage.

Because community events include people who aren't with us, we ask that a simple “filming in progress” note be posted near the entrance, and we'll gladly steer cameras away from anyone who'd rather not be filmed. Want copies afterward? Just ask — we're happy to share photos and clips for your recap, appreciation posts, or reporting.

20Community Sign-Ups

We may invite interested attendees to voluntarily share their contact info so we can let them know about future performances, free music programs, volunteer chances, and community events. It's always optional — no one ever has to sign up to enjoy the day. We keep that info to ourselves; we never sell or share it. We'd just ask for a little space around our table and banner, and that the sign-up spot isn't moved without a quick word to our lead.

21Safety, Security & Crowds

Overall event safety, security, guest behavior, crowd flow, emergency procedures, and venue rules are your team's domain — you know your event best. We look after our own crew and our agreed music, outreach, and filming role. If crowding, conflict, blocked exits, or any safety concern crops up near our area, we trust you'll respond promptly, and we may gently pause until it's resolved.

22Caring for Instruments & Belongings

Our musicians and volunteers bring instruments, cameras, tripods, stands, devices, outreach materials, and personal belongings — some of it valuable and dearly loved by our young people. We'll treat your space with great care, and we'd be grateful for a reasonably safe area for our things. We can't be responsible for damage caused by guests, weather, crowding, spills, or unauthorized handling.

24Cancellations & Rescheduling

If either of us needs to cancel or reschedule, we'll let the other know as soon as we possibly can, in writing. Life happens — illness, weather, emergencies, transportation — and we'll always give as much notice as we can. We can't take on losses or program gaps from circumstances outside our control, and if an event is rescheduled, our participation isn't automatic — a fresh quick confirmation may be needed. We'll always approach it generously.

28How You Can Talk About Us

We'd love a little thank-you! Accurate language is perfect — something like “Harmony 4 All joined us to support community connection through live music and community outreach.” Please don't describe us as a sponsor, vendor, endorser, an entertainment company, or a political or campaign supporter unless we've happily agreed in writing.

We're proudly nonpartisan. We'll gladly play at community, civic, and government-hosted events in the spirit of service, but our presence is never a political endorsement, and we can't perform, table, or provide media at campaign events, partisan events, candidate events, or political fundraisers unless our leadership has reviewed and approved it in writing.
